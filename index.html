Exhibit No. 01 — Platformer

Super Mario Bros.

Nintendo · 1985

Why it mattered

It single-handedly rescued the home console market after the 1983 industry crash by introducing precision physics, expansive side-scrolling world design, and a vibrant visual identity.

What came after

It established structural design paradigms for video game layout design, fluid physics loops, and secrets management that paved the way for subsequent titles like Sonic the Hedgehog and Rayman.

Exhibit No. 02 — First-person shooter

Doom

id Software · 1993

Why it mattered

It revolutionized PC computing by introducing fast-paced pseudo-3D graphics, immersive spatial audio design, open distribution through shareware, and pioneering network multiplayer options.

What came after

Its unprecedented commercial success spawned an entire generation of clones, initially called "Doom clones," laying down the codebase for Duke Nukem 3D, Quake, and modern tactical shooters.

Exhibit No. 03 — Role-playing game

World of Warcraft

Blizzard · 2004

Why it mattered

It streamlined historically punishing MMO mechanics by prioritizing seamless quest-driven leveling, accessible social matchmaking systems, and highly optimized server infrastructures.

What came after

It transformed online gaming into a mainstream entertainment industry, dictating the design loops, live-service patch cadences, and mechanics for successors like Final Fantasy XIV and Guild Wars 2.

Learn more on IGDB →
Exhibit No. 04 — Sandbox

Minecraft

Mojang · 2011

Why it mattered

It popularized systemic, emergent gameplay by ditching linear story narratives in favor of infinite, procedurally generated voxel terrains built entirely around player creativity and modular crafting.

What came after

It triggered a monumental industry shift toward user-generated content, directly inspiring survival-crafting blockbusters like Terraria and Ark: Survival Evolved, and foundational mechanics in games like Roblox.

Learn more on IGDB →
Exhibit No. 05 — Battle royale

PlayerUnknown's Battlegrounds

PUBG Corporation · 2017

Why it mattered

It successfully codified large-scale multiplayer survival tension by scaling a high-stakes, 100-player tactical simulation map, framed by a shrinking safe-zone mechanic.

What came after

Its explosive rise shifted the focus of global multiplayer development, forcing major studios to create giants like Fortnite, Apex Legends, and Call of Duty: Warzone.

Learn more on IGDB →
Exhibit No. 06 — Action-adventure / Soulslike

Dark Souls

FromSoftware · 2011

Why it mattered

It defiantly rejected the early-2010s trend of passive hand-holding by integrating deep, environmental storytelling with strict, deeply rewarding stamina-based combat loops.

What came after

It birthed a completely separate sub-genre classification known as "Soulslikes," reshaping third-person melee design across titles like Star Wars Jedi: Fallen Order, Hollow Knight, and Elden Ring.
